What we evaluate today

The question every project answers

Does our own model solve the problem better than the simplest alternative, with acceptable cost and risk?
🔧

Own model

Weights the group fine-tuned (full or LoRA) or trained, and evaluated. Prompting an API alone does not count.

⚖️

Fair comparison

Same held-out test set for the model and the baseline; decisions taken on validation, never on test.

🔍

Honest analysis

Beating the baseline is not required: a well-explained negative result beats a positive one without evidence.

[0:02 · 1 min] This is the guiding question of the project brief (section 1). Stress the last card: we grade the quality of the evaluation and the honesty of the analysis, not only the final number. The audience today is mixed (business and technical), like a real steering committee.

Rules check

Minute 9: your live Gradio demo throws an error and will not come back. What now?

A technical failure of the live demo is not penalised if you switch to the backup video, and the professor later verifies that the demo works with what you delivered. Showing only the video, without trying live, caps the demo criterion at "Bueno" (below 4.5).
[0:04 · 1 min] Quick poll in the chat (a, b, c or d), then click the majority answer. The point: always try live, but have the video one click away. Retraining live never fits in 3 minutes.

Individual defense

What the questions look like

  • “Your F1 improved after the iteration: on which split did you take that decision?”
  • “Why this baseline and not a stronger one?”
  • “Pick one error category: what is your hypothesis for it?”
  • “If a false negative cost twice as much, what would you change?”
  • “Where could personal data leak in your pipeline, and what did you do about it?”
  • “Tell me about one thing an AI assistant got wrong and how you caught it.”

Any member, any part. The question may be about a part you did not lead: everyone must be able to explain the whole project, including what an AI generated.

“I don't know, but I would verify it like this…” is a better answer than improvising.

[0:05 · 1 min] Optional if short on time. These are examples of the kind of questions, not the actual ones. Use the group's own numbers when you ask: the defense checks understanding and judgement, not memory. No AI assistants and no reading prepared answers during the defense.

Evaluación del proyecto · nota individual

Tu nota del proyecto

Nota del proyecto N = (15 × T + 10 × S + 5 × D) / 30 Nota individual del proyecto NI = N × F Aporte a la nota final = 0.30 × NI F = 0.7 + 0.3 × (P − 1) / 3, con tope en 1.0

P = promedio de los puntajes (1–5) que recibes de tus compañeros de grupo en la coevaluación (sin tu autoevaluación). F se redondea a dos decimales.

P (promedio recibido)1.01.52.02.53.03.5≥ 4.0
F0.700.750.800.850.900.951.00

T = entregable técnico: primero los descuentos por hitos (−0.3 por hito no entregado, máximo −1.2) y luego los topes (3.0 si no hay modelo propio). S = socialización y demo · D = defensa individual.

[0:07 · 1 min] Same formulas as rubric section 1 and section 5, word for word. Key message: with an average of 4 ("solid contribution") or more from your teammates you keep 100% of the project grade; below 4, each point less subtracts 0.1 from the factor, down to 0.7. The full worked example (group G03) is in rubric section 6.
Verifica

Tus dos compañeros te asignan en promedio P = 3.5 en la coevaluación. ¿Cuál es tu factor F?

F = 0.7 + 0.3 × (3.5 − 1) / 3 = 0.7 + 0.25 = 0.95. Solo con P ≥ 4.0 el factor llega a 1.00. Y si tú no entregas tu propia coevaluación, tu F baja 0.05 (sin bajar de 0.7).

Ejemplo de la rúbrica (G03, Ana): T = 4.10, S = 4.50, D = 4.00 → N = 4.2167 · P = 3.6 → F = 0.96 → NI = 4.05.

        Course recap

        Twelve sessions in one view

        01Introduction to Generative and Pretrained Models
        Generative models learn p(x) and sample from it. Prompting → RAG → fine-tuning → from scratch: more control, more cost.
        02Training Generative Models
        Loss, optimizer, learning rate; watch validation loss. A VAE trained on Fashion-MNIST.
        03Pretrained Models and Transfer Learning
        Attention and self-supervised pretraining; transfer with embeddings, few-shot or fine-tuning.
        04Fine-Tuning Generative Models
        Full fine-tuning vs LoRA (W = W₀ + BA): adapt a small model with curated examples.
        05Evaluating Generative Models
        BLEU, ROUGE, chrF, perplexity, F1 and error analysis: no single metric is enough.
        06Introduction to Basic Application Development
        From model to product: Gradio apps, confidence thresholds, human handoff, a first Startti agent.
        07Implementing Basic Chatbots and Agents
        From rules to LLM agents: conversation design, prompt injection, test conversations.
        08Building Classifiers with Generative Models
        Generative vs discriminative classifiers, zero-shot by label likelihood, synthetic data and its risks.
        09Use Cases: Generative Models in Real Contexts
        Value × feasibility, KPIs and ROI; documented failures teach as much as successes.
        10Ethics and Sustainability in Generative Models
        Bias, privacy (Ley 1581), regulation and carbon; model cards make them visible.
        11Evaluation and Continuous Model Improvement
        Error analysis → hypothesis → change → decision; robustness tests, quantization, monitoring.
        12Integrative Project
        Today: your own model, compared with a baseline, shown in a demo and defended.

        Foundations · Applications · Responsible & continuous · click a card to flip it.

        What's next

        Keep your project alive

        1. Publish the model card, and the model if the data allow it, on the Hugging Face Hub.
        2. Write a one-page go / no-go memo for your organization, with your numbers.
        3. Re-run your evaluation on fresh data in three months: data drift is real.
        4. Add the project to your portfolio, without private data or keys.

        A prototype becomes a product when someone owns the metric, the data and the risk. Name that person before you pitch it internally.
